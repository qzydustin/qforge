PESOSE Track 1 · Scoping a secure open-source ecosystem

The compiler is the trust boundary.

A quantum computer only returns trustworthy answers if the compiler that lowers a circuit to hardware preserves its behavior on a noisy device. Today that layer is fragmented across vendor stacks, and its noise models are blind to execution-dependent error. QForge turns robustness tooling into shared, secure, vendor-neutral infrastructure.

01 / The problem we scope

Robustness knowledge is stuck in one lab at a time.

Production quantum compilers model noise as calibration-derived costs on individual gates. They miss execution context: short, ordered gate sequences on specific physical-qubit neighborhoods that inflate error even when every gate is well calibrated. A four-gate pattern on a coupled pair, with no unusual per-gate error, measurably degrades results.

The seed tool, QRisk, isolates these recurring patterns from real hardware and links their removal to 24–45% lower excess error across three IBM Heron r2 backends. But a mitigation discovered in one lab does not become infrastructure the field can reuse and trust — there is no vendor-neutral pass interface, no governance, and no security model for a compiler pass whose correctness is statistical and drifts with calibration. QForge is a one-year effort to scope that ecosystem: architecture, governance and licensing, a security and provenance plan, and a community strategy.

03 / Building the ecosystem

From robustness tooling to shared infrastructure

Track 1 funds planning, not product: an open, vendor-neutral pass framework layered on existing interchange formats — a quantum IR (MLIR-style), OpenQASM 3, and QIR — plus the social structure a stochastic, hardware-coupled artifact needs before adoption scales.

01

Vendor-neutral pass interface

A pass contract that separates portable transformation logic from backend-specific evidence, plugging into vendor pass managers such as Qiskit and t|ket⟩ rather than competing as a new IR.

02

Governance & licensing

An open, meritocratic charter and decision-rights matrix, a permissive core license paired with a Developer Certificate of Origin, and a data-sharing agreement for calibration-derived patterns.

03

Security & provenance

A threat model for six risk classes, an equivalence oracle plus N-of-M independent cross-window rediscovery as the trust floor, and every accepted pass signed and published with an SBOM and validation evidence.

04

Community & workforce

Explicit user and contributor funnels, a Robustness Pass Challenge built on QASMBench and the pattern database, and research experiences across three universities, two of them Hispanic-Serving Institutions.

04 / The ecosystem

Layered on what already exists.

QForge is not another compiler. It layers on the interfaces vendors already ship — one shared pass abstraction above Qiskit, t|ket⟩, and the NISQ hardware beneath them.

A robustness mitigation written once against that abstraction runs everywhere. The ecosystem serves algorithm researchers, tool vendors, national labs and HPC centers, and educators — anyone who needs a compiler pass they did not write to be one they can trust.
